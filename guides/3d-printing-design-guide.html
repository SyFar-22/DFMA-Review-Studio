3D printing design guide: technologies, materials and limits

The seven families of 3D printing, 14 technologies with build size, minimum wall and hole, supports and materials.

The seven families of 3D printing

ISO/ASTM 52900
Material extrusion

A nozzle lays down melted plastic (FDM, FFF) or metal-filled filament. Cheap and common; visible layers; weakest between layers.

Vat photopolymerisation

Light hardens liquid resin layer by layer (SLA laser, DLP projector, MSLA screen, Carbon DLS). Fine detail and smooth surfaces.

Powder bed fusion

A laser or electron beam melts powder (SLS and MJF for nylon; DMLS, SLM, EBM for metal). Strong parts; polymer needs no supports, metal does.

Material jetting

Print heads jet droplets of resin and cure them (PolyJet). Full colour and multi-material prototypes.

Binder jetting

Glue is printed into powder; metal parts are then sintered and shrink about 20 %. Fast for many small metal parts, also used for sand casting moulds.

Directed energy deposition

Wire or powder is melted as it is deposited (DED, LMD, WAAM). Large near-net metal parts and repairs, always machined after.

Sheet lamination

Sheets are bonded and cut (LOM, ultrasonic). Rare today.

Design rules for 3D printing

  1. Respect the minimum wall and hole.Below the minimum, walls break or do not print and small holes close up. The table above lists them per technology.
  2. Orient for strength and finish.FDM parts are weakest between layers: put loads along the layers. Faces facing down onto supports are rougher.
  3. Avoid unsupported overhangs.FDM, resin and metal powder need supports past about 45 to 60°; supports leave marks and add cost. Nylon powder and MJF need none.
  4. Let powder and resin out.Hollow parts need two escape holes (at least Ø3 to 5 mm) so trapped powder or resin can be removed.
  5. Plan finishing for tight features.Bearing seats, threads and sealing faces are usually drilled, reamed, tapped or machined after printing.
  6. Use the freedom.Merge parts, add internal channels and lattices, and remove fasteners. That is where printing beats machining on cost.
